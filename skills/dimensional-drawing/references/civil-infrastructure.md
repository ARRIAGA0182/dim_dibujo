# Civil engineering and infrastructure drawing reference

Use this guide for site/civil works, roads, grading, drainage, water supply, sewerage, and utility drawings. Preserve the provenance and accuracy of survey and design data.

## Typical deliverables

- Existing/proposed site plan, grading plan, contours, spot elevations, earthwork limits, and cross-sections.
- Road alignment, horizontal/vertical geometry, stationing, longitudinal profile, cross-sections, and typical sections.
- Water-supply, sewer, stormwater, and other utility plans and profiles with node/structure IDs and connectivity.
- Detail sheets for manholes, chambers, culverts, inlets, valves, hydrants, thrust restraint, and other components only when source specifications are available.
- Quantity or schedule tables tied to the same alignment, geometry, and revision.

## Survey and coordinate data

- Preserve the coordinate reference system (CRS), horizontal datum, vertical datum, units, and source accuracy. If unknown, label [TBD]; do not guess the CRS from the appearance of a map.
- Keep surveyed/existing terrain distinct from proposed terrain. Do not invent contours, property boundaries, utility locations, or elevations.
- Record the source of stationing and any station equations or alignment offsets.
- Make horizontal and vertical scales explicit on profiles; do not imply they are the same if vertical exaggeration is used.

## Profiles and networks

For a profile, coordinate station, existing-ground elevation, proposed/design elevation, slope/grade, vertical geometry, structure locations, and invert/center elevations as relevant.
For water/drainage networks, preserve node/structure IDs, connectivity, pipe diameter, material, pressure/rating class, slope, cover, invert levels, and hydraulic values only when supplied or calculated from a verified method and inputs.
Use clear labels to distinguish schematic network diagrams from scaled, georeferenced plans.

## Coordination checks

Check:
- Coordinate and elevation units, CRS/datum, stationing, and scale.
- Profile stations against plan alignment.
- Slopes and elevation differences against the stated calculation method.
- Pipe/structure connectivity and IDs between plan, profile, and schedules.
- Existing versus proposed levels, crossings, clearances, and conflict notes when data exists.
- Quantity tables against the same geometry and revision.

Do not claim hydraulic, geotechnical, traffic, road-design, or regulatory compliance from drafting checks alone. Mark missing survey/design inputs [TBD] or [VERIFY].
