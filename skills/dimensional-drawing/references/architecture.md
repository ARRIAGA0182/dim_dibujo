# Architecture drawing reference

Use this guide when the requested deliverable is architectural or architectural-construction documentation. Follow the project's own drafting standard and applicable local regulations; this guide is not a substitute for a jurisdiction-specific code review.

## Recommended drawing set

Choose only the views needed for the task:
- Site/context plan: property limits, north, access, existing features, topography, setbacks, and site dimensions when verified.
- Floor plan(s): axes, overall and locating dimensions, wall openings, doors/windows, stairs, room labels, levels, and room/area schedule as required.
- Roof plan: roof edges, ridge/valley lines, slope arrows, drainage and access where specified.
- Elevations: finished-floor and other key levels, openings, roof profile, and material/finish notes only when supplied.
- Sections: vertical relationships, floor-to-floor heights, roof build-up, ground line, stair geometry, and references to enlarged details.
- Enlarged details: junctions, openings, stairs, wet areas, roofs, parapets, and envelope assemblies.
- Schedules: rooms/areas, doors, windows, finishes, or other repeated components when relevant.

## Dimensioning

- Establish a clear hierarchy: overall dimensions, grid/axis dimensions, locating dimensions, opening dimensions, and local detail dimensions.
- Avoid duplicate dimensions that can conflict. Dimension from consistent datums and axes.
- Show units and distinguish finished dimensions from rough openings or structural dimensions when relevant.
- Use actual values only when supplied or derived from verified geometry. Do not infer a wall thickness, opening size, setback, or ceiling height from a screenshot without identifying it as an estimate.
- Make plan, elevation, section, schedule, and detail dimensions agree. Report any mismatch rather than hiding it.
- Areas must state the measurement basis where it matters (gross, net, covered, usable, or other project-defined category).

## Graphic hierarchy

- Cut elements: strongest lineweight.
- Visible elements beyond the cut: medium lineweight.
- Fixtures, furniture, overhead elements, and background context: lighter or dashed according to the project convention.
- Dimensions, leaders, grids, levels, and hatches: legible but subordinate to primary geometry.
- Existing, demolished, and proposed work must be distinguishable in a legend when present.

## Coordination and validation

Check:
- Exterior dimensions against internal dimension chains.
- Door/window locations and schedules.
- Stair direction, landings, floor-to-floor height, and geometry from supplied data.
- Alignment of openings and levels across elevations and sections.
- Section/detail references and sheet numbers.
- Area totals against the geometry and the stated area basis.
- North arrow, scale, site datum, and orientation where applicable.
