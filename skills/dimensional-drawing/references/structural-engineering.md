# Structural engineering drawing reference

Use this guide for structural layouts and details based on supplied or verified engineering design information. A drawing check is not a structural analysis or professional approval.

## Typical deliverables

- Foundation/footing layout and footing, pedestal, column, beam, slab, wall, stair, or retaining-structure details.
- Steel framing, member marks, connections, bracing, base plates, and bolt/weld callouts when defined by the design.
- Reinforcement plans, elevations, sections, bar marks, schedules, and bending details when the source includes the necessary data.
- General notes and material schedules only when provided or verified for the project.

## Required source data

Use approved drawings, structural model information, design calculations, member schedules, specifications, and engineer instructions. Depending on the task, critical inputs may include loads, spans, support conditions, material strengths, soil/foundation criteria, exposure, cover, seismic criteria, and applicable design code/edition.

If a detail depends on missing data, mark it [TBD] or [VERIFY]. Do not fill gaps with generic typical details.

## Drawing conventions

- Keep grid/axis references, member tags, levels, dimensions, section marks, and detail references consistent across all views.
- Distinguish concrete outlines, steel profiles, visible/hidden edges, centerlines, cut sections, and reinforcement using the project line and hatch conventions.
- Reinforcement notes must identify only verified bar diameter/grade, spacing, quantity, location, hooks, laps, anchorage, and cover.
- Keep bar marks unique and coordinated between the drawing and schedule.
- If showing bar lengths or quantities, state the calculation basis and account for bend allowances, hooks, laps, and waste only where specified.
- Do not silently convert between bar diameter, nominal designation, gauge, or imperial/metric units.

## Coordination checks

Check:
- Member dimensions and tags between plan, elevation, section, and schedule.
- Reinforcement count, direction, spacing, layer, and termination against the source detail.
- Openings, embeds, sleeves, and service penetrations against coordinated drawings.
- Levels and support conditions at member intersections.
- Material grades, notes, and detail callouts against the project specification.
- Any dimension-chain closure or quantity discrepancy; report the mismatch.

Never infer that a member is safe because the drawing is geometrically consistent. Structural capacity, stability, detailing adequacy, and code compliance require appropriate engineering calculations and review.
